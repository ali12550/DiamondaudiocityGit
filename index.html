Definition: Pitch Detectors is a free, browser-based collection of audio tools and calculators for musicians, producers, podcasters, and engineers. It covers tempo and rhythm, pitch and tuning, music theory, sound analysis, audio editing, speaker design, and audiobooks — every tool computing instantly on your device with no installation or account.

How the Tools Work: Accuracy and Privacy

Every tool is built on the Web Audio API, the W3C standard for sound in the browser. Your browser decodes the audio, runs the analysis or edit, and encodes the result — all on your device. Microphone tools use the browser's getUserMedia permission; the audio stream never leaves your machine.

This architecture is the privacy story: because nothing is uploaded, there is nothing to store, leak, or hand over. It is also the business model — with no server compute or storage cost per user, the tools stay free without accounts or paywalls.

On accuracy: tuners use autocorrelation pitch detection and are typically precise within a couple of cents on a clear signal. The decibel meter is the honest exception — phone microphones are not calibrated to a reference level, so readings can differ by several decibels between devices. Treat it as a useful estimate for spotting loud environments, not as a compliance instrument.

Each tool page documents its exact formula or standard, and calculation tools are verified against hand calculations and published reference tables before release.

Limitations

  • Single-purpose tools, not a DAW. There is no multitrack mixing, EQ, or effects chain here.
  • Memory-bounded editing. Audio files are decoded into RAM, so very long high-resolution recordings may strain phones.
  • Estimates, not instruments. The decibel meter cannot replace a calibrated meter for occupational noise compliance.
  • Planning-grade results. Calculators give solid answers for learning and everyday decisions, not certified engineering measurements.
