Advanced Concepts in Note to Frequency

Understanding the relationship between notes and frequencies is crucial for music theory, acoustic analysis, and electronic sound design. Notes are quantified in MIDI values, while frequencies define the physical oscillations per second (Hz). Knowledge of semitones, cents, octaves, and pitch ratios allows precise control over harmonics and tuning.

  • Cents: Fine measurement between a note and its exact frequency.
  • Semitones: 12 equal divisions per octave in 12-TET tuning.
  • Pitch Classes: Notes grouped by name regardless of octave (C, C#, D, etc.).
  • Frequency Ratios: Doubling frequency raises pitch by one octave, 2^(1/12) per semitone.

The Formula

Frequency Calculation:
f = 440 × 2^((n-69)/12)

Where f is the frequency in Hz and n is the MIDI note number (A4 = 69).

Alternative Formula:
f = 440 × 2^((semitones from A4)/12)

Calculate semitones from A4, then apply the power of 2 relationship.

Key Relationships

  • Octave: Doubling the frequency raises pitch by one octave
  • Semitone: Multiply by 2^(1/12) ≈ 1.0595 for one semitone up
  • Fifth: Multiply by 2^(7/12) ≈ 1.498 for a perfect fifth
