Mastering Guitar Tuning: The Physics of String Vibration, Harmonics, and Pitch Stability

Tuning a guitar is an acoustic calibration that aligns string vibrations with mathematical pitch intervals. When you pluck an open guitar string, it produces a fundamental standing wave along its speaking length ($L$, the distance from nut to bridge saddle) whose frequency is governed by Mersenne's Law:

$$f = \frac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$$

Where $T$ is string tension (in Newtons or pounds of pull) and $\mu$ is linear mass density (the thickness/gauge of the string). Turning a tuning peg increases tension $T$, pulling pitch sharp; loosening the peg decreases tension, dropping pitch flat.

Because guitar strings are non-ideal physical wires with stiffness and overtones, standard human hearing can struggle to distinguish the true fundamental frequency from bright metallic upper harmonics. This Online Guitar Tuner applies a 4096-sample time-domain autocorrelation algorithm to strip away harmonic distortion and lock onto the true fundamental string frequency with cent-level precision.

Professional Guitar Tuning Best Practices

1. Always Tune UP to Pitch

Never tune down into a note. If a string is sharp, detune it half a turn BELOW the target pitch, then slowly tune UP into the green center. This prevents slack in the tuning gear box from slipping when you bend notes.

2. Mute Sympathetic Strings

When tuning a single string, rest your palm lightly across the remaining five strings. Unmuted open strings will vibrate sympathetically (acoustic resonance), confusing the microphone pitch analyzer with multiple frequencies.

3. Check 12th-Fret Intonation

Tune the open string to 0 cents, then lightly touch the string over the 12th fret wire to play a 12th-fret harmonic. Next, press down and play the 12th fretted note. If the fretted note is sharp compared to the harmonic, your bridge saddle needs to be adjusted back.

In standard concert tuning (A4 = 440 Hz), 6-string guitar open strings are tuned to:
  • 6th String (Low E) = E2 (82.41 Hz)
  • 5th String = A2 (110.00 Hz)
  • 4th String = D3 (146.83 Hz)
  • 3rd String = G3 (196.00 Hz)
  • 2nd String = B3 (246.94 Hz)
  • 1st String (High E) = E4 (329.63 Hz)

Guitar tuning machine heads (gears and posts) contain subtle mechanical slack (backlash). If you tune down to a note, friction holds the gear teeth loosely; as soon as you bend a string or strum hard, the slack releases and the string drops flat. By tuning down below the pitch and tuning UP into the target, string tension locks the tuning post gears securely.

Unplugged solid-body electric guitars produce quiet acoustic string vibrations. To tune an unplugged electric guitar, sit in a quiet room and hold the guitar body or headstock directly within 6 to 12 inches of your laptop or phone microphone. Pluck the string firmly with a pick or thumb.

Half Step Down lowers all six strings by one semitone (Eb-Ab-Db-Gb-Bb-Eb) for reduced string tension and warmer vocal accompaniment. DADGAD creates an open Celtic modal tuning (D2-A2-D3-G3-A3-D4) with rich droning resonances.
