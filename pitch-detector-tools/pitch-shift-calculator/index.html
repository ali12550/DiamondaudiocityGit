The Physics and Mathematics of Pitch Shifting

Pitch shifting alters the perceived fundamental frequency ($F_0$) of an acoustic or digital audio signal. In musical acoustics, human pitch perception is fundamentally logarithmic rather than linear. Pitch intervals correspond to mathematical frequency ratios, defined by the formula:

$$r = 2^{ rac{S + rac{C}{100}}{12}} = 2^{ rac{S}{12}} imes 2^{ rac{C}{1200}}$$

Where $S$ represents semitone shift and $C$ represents fine microtonal cents. When an audio frequency $f_{in}$ is transposed, the resulting frequency is:

$$f_{out} = f_{in} imes r$$

Because Western music relies predominantly on 12-Tone Equal Temperament (12-TET), each semitone represents an exact frequency multiplication factor of $\sqrt[12]{2} \approx 1.059463094$. Shifting up $12$ semitones multiplies all frequencies by $2.0$ (one octave), while shifting down $12$ semitones cuts all frequencies in half ($0.5$).

Varispeed vs. Modern Time-Independent Pitch Shifting

1. Varispeed (Tape / Turntable Resampling)

Historically, pitch shifting on analog tape machines (like the Studer A800 or Ampex ATR-102) and DJ vinyl turntables (Technics SL-1200) was achieved by changing motor speed. Pitch and duration are mathematically coupled:

  • Tempo change: $\text{New BPM} = \text{Original BPM} \times r$
  • Audio duration: $\text{New Length} = \frac{\text{Original Length}}{r}$
  • Playback speed shift (%): $\Delta \text{Speed} = (r - 1) \times 100\%$

Resampling introduces zero phase smearing, transient blurring, or robotic DSP artifacts, making it the preferred method for classic hip-hop and jungle sample chopping.

2. Time-Independent Pitch Shifting (DSP)

Modern Digital Audio Workstations (DAWs) use sophisticated algorithms to alter pitch without changing sample length or playback tempo:

  • Phase Vocoder: Converts audio into Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) frequency bins, scales bin frequencies, and resynthesizes time frames.
  • WSOLA (Waveform Similarity Overlap-Add): Slices waveform into overlapping grains and repeats/skips cycles in time domain to decouple speed from pitch.
  • Granular Resynthesis: Breaks sound into micro-grains (10–50ms) to reconstruct timbre.

Time-independent shifting preserves project tempo synchronization at the cost of potential phase dispersion and transient softening.

Formants & Formant Preservation Explained

A fundamental challenge in vocal pitch shifting is the acoustic formant. When humans sing or speak, the vocal cords generate a harmonic spectrum, but the geometry of the throat, mouth, and nasal cavity (the vocal tract) acts as a physical acoustic filter, amplifying distinct resonant frequency bands called formants ($F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4$).

Why Formants Cause the "Chipmunk Effect":

Formants do not shift when a person sings different notes; vocal tract length remains physically constant. However, standard pitch shifting shifts both the fundamental frequency and the formant spectral peaks upward. This transforms an adult vocal timbre into the acoustic profile of a tiny infant or animated rodent (the classic "chipmunk effect"). Conversely, pitching down without formant preservation creates the sluggish "Darth Vader" timbre.

Formant-Corrected Pitch Shifting:

Advanced algorithms (such as zplane elastique Pro, Melodyne DNA, and IRCAM SuperVP) separate the harmonic excitation spectrum from the spectral envelope filter. The excitation pitch is transposed by ratio $r$, while the formant filter envelope remains anchored at its original anatomical frequencies, preserving natural human timbre.

Step-by-Step Worked Production Calculations

Example 1: Repitching a Vocal Hook in Ableton Live / FL Studio

Scenario: You import a female vocal sample in $A\text{ minor}$ into a beat written in $C\text{ minor}$. You need to pitch the sample up by $3\text{ semitones}$. What is the frequency ratio, and how does it affect sample speed if pitched in Re-Pitch (varispeed) mode?

  1. Identify semitone shift: $S = +3$, $C = 0$.
  2. Compute frequency ratio: $r = 2^{3/12} = 2^{0.25} \approx 1.189207$.
  3. Determine varispeed playback acceleration: $\Delta \text{Speed} = (1.189207 - 1) \times 100 = +18.92\%$.
  4. If original sample tempo was $120\text{ BPM}$, the repitched tempo becomes: $120 \times 1.189207 = 142.70\text{ BPM}$.
Production Decision: If you want the sample to stay at $120\text{ BPM}$, use Complex Pro mode with Formants set to $100\%$ to preserve natural vocal timbre. If you desire a classic pitched-up house/drill hook, use Re-Pitch mode at $142.7\text{ BPM}$.
Example 2: Analog Synthesizer Unison Detuning

Scenario: In a softsynth (Serum/Vital), you set Oscillator 2 to detune by $+18\text{ cents}$ above Oscillator 1 playing note $A_3$ ($220.00\text{ Hz}$). What is Oscillator 2's frequency, and what is the resulting acoustic beat frequency?

  1. Base frequency $f_1 = 220.00\text{ Hz}$, $S = 0$, $C = +18\text{ cents}$.
  2. Compute pitch ratio: $r = 2^{18 / 1200} = 2^{0.015} \approx 1.010452$.
  3. Oscillator 2 frequency: $f_2 = 220.00 \times 1.010452 = 222.30\text{ Hz}$.
  4. Acoustic beat throbbing frequency: $f_{\text{beat}} = |222.30 - 220.00| = 2.30\text{ Hz}$ (2.3 volume pulses per second).
Sound Design Result: The two oscillators will produce a rich, animated chorus pulsation at $2.30\text{ Hz}$ without causing phase cancellation or harsh dissonance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Pitch shifting follows an exponential logarithmic ratio. Shifting pitch by $S$ semitones and $C$ cents produces a frequency multiplication ratio $r = 2^{(S + C/100) / 12}$. For example, shifting upward by 7 semitones (a perfect fifth) yields a ratio of $2^{7/12} \approx 1.4983$, meaning every frequency component of the sound is multiplied by 1.4983.

Varispeed (analog tape or turntable pitching) couples pitch and playback speed together: pitching up speeds up the tempo and shortens audio duration. Modern time-independent pitch shifting uses digital signal processing (such as phase vocoders or WSOLA algorithms) to alter pitch while maintaining the exact original tempo and duration.

The chipmunk effect occurs when pitch is shifted upward without formant preservation. Formants are the fixed acoustic resonances of the human vocal tract that define vocal identity and vowel timbre. When simple resampling or pitch shifting scales these formants upward along with fundamental pitch, the voice sounds like a tiny, childlike speaker. Formant-preserving algorithms maintain vocal tract resonant frequencies regardless of pitch shift.

When repitching a sample (vinyl/tape style), the new BPM equals Original BPM multiplied by the pitch ratio $r = 2^{\text{semitones} / 12}$. The percentage speed change is $((r - 1) \times 100)\%$. For example, shifting a 120 BPM sample up 2 semitones gives $r = 2^{2/12} \approx 1.12246$, resulting in a new tempo of 134.70 BPM (+12.25% speed increase).

Exactly 100 cents equal one equal-tempered semitone. An entire octave consists of 12 semitones or 1200 cents. Cents allow sound engineers and musicians to perform fine micro-tuning adjustments, such as detuning synth oscillators by +15 cents or correcting a vocalist who is 20 cents flat.

In Ableton Live, use Complex or Complex Pro (with formant control) for full mixes and vocals, Beats for drums, and Re-Pitch for tape-style varispeed. In Logic Pro or Pro Tools, use Polyphonic for chords, Monophonic for solo instruments, and X-Form or Elastique Pro for the highest quality polyphonic rendering without phase smearing.
