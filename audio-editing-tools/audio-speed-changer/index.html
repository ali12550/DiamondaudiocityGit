What Is an Audio Speed Changer?

An audio speed changer multiplies the playback rate of a recording by a factor S: the duration becomes 1/S of the original, and — in varispeed mode — every pitch rises by 12 × log₂(S) semitones. It is the digital descendant of the varispeed knob on reel-to-reel tape machines.

Two different technologies share this name, and confusing them causes most disappointment. Varispeed (this tool) changes speed the honest physical way: faster playback means the waveform completes more cycles per second, so pitch must rise. Time-stretching (podcast apps, DJ software) uses DSP like WSOLA or phase vocoders to change tempo while holding pitch steady. If you want the Alvin-and-the-Chipmunks effect, you want varispeed. If you want to finish a podcast faster without squeaky voices, you want time-stretch — a different tool.

Methodology: the live preview sets player.playbackRate = S on the HTML audio element (range 0.25–4, step 0.05). Speed-to-Pitch Formula and Worked Example

Varispeed pitch shift follows a clean logarithmic law — the same math as musical octaves:

Pitch shift (semitones) = 12 × log₂(S)

Worked example: a guitar riff in A major played at 1.5×:

  1. Pitch shift: 12 × log₂(1.5) = 12 × 0.585 = +7.02 semitones (a perfect fifth plus a hair)
  2. The A (110 Hz) root becomes 110 × 1.5 = 165 Hz — roughly an E
  3. Duration: a 4:00 solo becomes 4:00 ÷ 1.5 = 2:40
  4. At 0.5×: 12 × log₂(0.5) = −12 semitones — the same riff one octave lower, lasting 8:00

The piano analogy: each speed doubling climbs exactly 12 keys. 2× from middle C lands on the C above; 4× lands two octaves up. This is why 2× voices sound "chipmunk" — human hearing reads a full-octave jump as a different-sized speaker.

The Transcriptionist's Speed Ladder

Professional transcriptionists don't just pick 0.5× and suffer — they climb a ladder. Start at 0.7× for a first pass: speech stays intelligible (pitch drops only ~6 semitones) while giving your fingers time to keep up. Drop to 0.5× for dense passages, crosstalk, or heavy accents — one full octave down, every syllable exposed. For review, climb to 1.25×: errors jump out because your brain hears the text at an unfamiliar rate.

One caution: at 0.5× and below, varispeed pitch drop makes voices sound unnaturally deep, which can blur consonants like s and f. If consonants matter more than vowels (medical, legal transcription), a pitch-preserving time-stretcher serves better than this varispeed tool.

Limitations

  • Preview and download sound different. The in-browser preview uses the audio element's default pitch correction, so 2× preview keeps the original pitch. The downloaded WAV is true varispeed — 2× export really is an octave higher. Judge the final result from the download, not the preview.
  • Pitch always moves with speed. There is no pitch-preservation mode here; for chipmunk-free speedup use a time-stretching app instead.
  • Extreme rates degrade clarity. At 4×, consonants smear and reverb tails turn to mush; at 0.25×, low voices fall below comfortable hearing range.
  • Output is always WAV. The download is an uncompressed 16-bit WAV — larger than the input MP3. Convert afterward for sharing.
  • Entire file is processed in memory. Very long files are best handled on desktop browsers.
