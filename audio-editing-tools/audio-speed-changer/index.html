Audio Speed Changer: Technical Guide to Playback Rate, Varispeed & Time-Domain Resampling

Master the physics and digital signal processing mechanics of audio speed manipulation. Explore how playback rate scaling impacts musical pitch, temporal duration, sample interpolation, and frequency spectrum distribution.

1. The Physics of Playback Speed & Varispeed Resampling

In both physical acoustics and digital signal processing, altering the speed of an audio recording changes the rate at which discrete samples are presented to the listener.

Historically, this was achieved on magnetic reel-to-reel tape machines and direct-drive vinyl turntables by varying the motor rotation speed—a technique known as varispeed. In digital audio, varispeed is executed through time-domain sample rate conversion (resampling):

Duration Scaling

The total duration of the track scales inversely with the playback speed factor $S$:

T_{\text{new}} = \frac{T_{\text{original}}}{S}

At $S = 2.0\times$, a 4-minute song plays in exactly 2 minutes.

Pitch & Frequency Scaling

Every frequency component $f$ shifts in direct linear proportion to $S$:

f_{\text{new}} = S \cdot f_{\text{original}}

At $S = 2.0\times$, a $440\text{ Hz}$ A4 fundamental rises to $880\text{ Hz}$ (A5).

2. The Semitone Pitch Shift Formula

Because Western musical pitch is perceived logarithmically according to the 12-tone equal temperament scale (12-TET), doubling the frequency corresponds to an increase of 12 semitones (one octave). The precise pitch deviation $\Delta m$ in semitones caused by any speed multiplier $S$ is expressed as:

\Delta m = 12 \cdot \log_2(S) = 12 \cdot \frac{\ln(S)}{\ln(2)} \approx 39.863 \cdot \log_{10}(S)

Conversely, if you know the desired musical interval shift $\Delta m$, the required speed factor $S$ is calculated by:

S = 2^{\frac{\Delta m}{12}}

4. Varispeed Resampling vs. Time-Stretching Algorithms

When altering audio playback speed, engineers face a fundamental choice between classic tape varispeed and digital time-stretching:

DSP Method Pitch Impact Transient Integrity Artifact Vulnerability Primary Applications
Varispeed Resampling (This Tool) Pitch shifts proportionally with speed ($f \propto S$) 100% Crisp Transients Zero phase smearing; classic analog character Nightcore, Chopped & Screwed, hip-hop sample pitching, vinyl scratch emulation.
WSOLA (Waveform Similarity Overlap-Add) Pitch preserved ($f$ constant) Moderate transient blur Stuttering or flanging on complex polyphonic chords Voice dictation, podcast playback (1.5x / 2x speech comprehension).
Phase Vocoder (STFT) Pitch preserved ($f$ constant) Softened drum attacks Phasiness, metallic ringing, and transient smearing DAW elastic audio, sound design, ambient drone stretching.

Music Genre Aesthetics

Creating Nightcore (speeding dance tracks up to 1.25x–1.35x for anime/hyperpop vocal brightness) and Chopped & Screwed / Vaporwave (slowing funk and R&B tracks to 0.7x–0.85x for deep, slurred bass aesthetics).

Instrument Practice & Transcription

Slowing down blistering guitar solos, bebop saxophone riffs, or intricate drum rudiments to 0.5x or 0.75x allows musicians to transcribe notes accurately and practice along with gradual tempo ramp-ups.

Podcast & Audio Learning

Consuming informational spoken-word content, university lectures, and audiobooks at 1.25x or 1.5x to reduce listening time while preserving vocal intelligibility.

Choreography & Rehearsal

Adjusting dance performance tracks and theatre backing audio to practice complex choreography at comfortable slow tempos before returning to full show speed.
