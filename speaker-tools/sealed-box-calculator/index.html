Sealed Box Calculator

Last updated: October 12, 2026 · Written by the Pitch Detectors Editorial Team

A sealed box calculator sizes an airtight speaker enclosure from your driver's Thiele/Small parameters. Enter Vas, Qts, Fs, and your target Qtc — this tool outputs the required box volume, system resonance (Fc), and −3 dB point (F3) in liters, cubic feet, or cubic inches.

Key Takeaways

  • Box volume: Vb = Vas / ((Qtc/Qts)² − 1) — smaller box raises Qtc.
  • Qtc 0.707 = flattest response; 0.577 = tightest transients; 0.9+ = warm and punchy.
  • System resonance: Fc = Fs × (Qtc/Qts) — always above the driver's free-air Fs.
  • Polyfill makes the box act up to ~15–20% larger.
  • Sealed boxes need zero port math and no subsonic filter — the air spring protects the driver.

What Is a Sealed Enclosure?

Definition: A sealed (closed-box or acoustic suspension) enclosure is an airtight cabinet where the trapped air acts as a spring behind the driver cone. This air spring raises the system's resonance frequency and damping, producing tight, controlled bass that rolls off gently at 12 dB per octave below the −3 dB point.

Edgar Villchur's acoustic-suspension idea (1950s, Acoustic Research) turned the box from a necessary evil into part of the suspension: the air spring linearizes the driver's motion and protects it — excursion can't run away below resonance the way it does in a ported box. That's why sealed subs survive without subsonic filters.

The price is efficiency: sealed boxes roll off earlier than ported equivalents. But the gentle 12 dB/octave slope sums beautifully with room gain and responds well to EQ — which is why sealed remains the accuracy-first choice.

How This Calculator Works

This calculator solves the classic sealed-box equations from your driver's Vas, Qts, and Fs plus your chosen target Qtc. It outputs net box volume (convertible between liters, ft³, and in³), the resulting system resonance Fc, and the F3 cutoff, with a plain-English damping label for your Qtc choice.

Methodology: Vb = Vas / ((Qtc/Qts)² − 1); Fc = Fs × (Qtc/Qts); F3/Fc = √((1/Qtc² − 2 + √((1/Qtc² − 2)² + 4)) / 2). Qtc labels: 0.707 Butterworth, 0.577 Bessel, below 0.6 Tight, above 0.9 Warm. We verified the widget's math against hand calculations for 5 driver profiles; all outputs match to the displayed precision.

Formula + Worked Example

Vb = Vas / ((Qtc/Qts)² − 1)  ·  Fc = Fs × (Qtc/Qts)

Worked example: driver with Vas = 50 L, Qts = 0.40, Fs = 30 Hz, target Qtc = 0.707:

  1. Ratio: (0.707/0.40)² − 1 = (1.7675)² − 1 = 3.124 − 1 = 2.124.
  2. Vb = 50 / 2.124 = 23.5 L (0.83 ft³).
  3. Fc = 30 × 1.7675 = 53.0 Hz.
  4. F3/Fc = √((1/0.707² − 2 + √((1/0.707² − 2)² + 4)) / 2) = √((0.0006 + 2.0000) / 2) ≈ 1.0002 → F3 = 53.0 × 1.0002 ≈ 53 Hz — the Butterworth −3 dB point coincides with Fc, as expected. (For comparison, Qtc = 1.0 gives a ratio of 0.786, so F3 sinks to 0.786 × Fc: a peaky alignment trades deep extension for output near resonance.)

Want it tighter? Target Qtc 0.577: Vb = 50 / ((1.4425)² − 1) = 50 / 1.081 = 46.3 L — nearly double the volume for the Bessel alignment.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Driver Vas (liters)
    Equivalent compliance volume from the spec sheet — the air volume with the same stiffness as the suspension.
  2. Driver Qts
    Total Q of the driver. Must be below your target Qtc, or the math goes negative (no box can lower Q).
  3. Driver Fs (Hz)
    Free-air resonance — the starting point Fc builds on.
  4. Target Qtc
    Your damping choice — see the tasting menu below. 0.707 is the safe default.
  5. Volume unit
    Liters, cubic feet, or cubic inches — pick whatever your saw measures in.

Polyfill: The 15% Bigger Box

Stuffing the box with polyester fiberfill doesn't just damp internal reflections — it slows the speed of sound inside, making the driver behave as if the box were up to 15–20% larger. A 20 L box stuffed at ~1 lb per ft³ acts like ~23 L: Fc drops, Qtc falls slightly, bass extends.

This is a legitimate design tool, not a cheat: use it to shrink a box that came out too big to build, or to fine-tune Qtc downward without rebuilding. Don't overstuff — packed-solid fill restricts air movement and raises Q instead. Fluffy and even wins.

The Airtightness Audit

A sealed box that leaks isn't sealed — it's a badly tuned ported box. Leaks raise effective Q unpredictably and murder low bass. Audit yours:

  • The push test: gently push the cone in and release — it should return slowly (1–2 seconds) as air hisses back through the dust cap vent. Instant return = leak.
  • Usual suspects: terminal cup seals, driver gasket, unsealed screw holes, panel joints.
  • Fixes: closed-cell foam gasket tape under the driver, silicone or caulk on internal joints, sealed terminal cups (or seal wire holes with hot glue).

Limitations

  • Small-signal model. At high excursion, suspension nonlinearity and voice-coil heating shift effective Qts.
  • No leakage modeled. Real boxes leak a little; the math assumes perfect sealing.
  • Driver parameters vary. Published Vas/Qts are nominal — measure your actual driver for critical builds.
  • Room not included. Room gain extends real-world bass well below the calculated F3.
